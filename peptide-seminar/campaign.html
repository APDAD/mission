feed the patient properly while they are on it.

3 · What just happened federally

Send Mon Aug 17Everyone

The news hook

Subject line
What the FDA panel actually voted on in July, and what it did not
The peptide rules are moving again. Here is where it really stands.
Body
Hope you are well.

There is a lot of noise right now about peptides coming back, and some of
it is wrong in a way that could get a practitioner in trouble. So here is
the clean version.

In late July an FDA advisory committee voted to recommend six peptides,
including BPC-157, for the compounding list. That is real and it is a
meaningful shift in direction.

It is also advisory. Nothing changed in law. Nothing that was off the
table in June is on the table today, and the rulemaking that would
actually change it takes many months. If you are seeing vendors claim
BPC-157 is cleared, they are ahead of the facts.

Meanwhile the practical situation has not eased. Compounded semaglutide
and tirzepatide are gone since the shortage ended, so patients who were
paying a couple hundred a month are now looking at brand pricing or
quitting. A lot of them are quitting, and quitting has its own rebound
problem.

And here in Georgia the board has been running unannounced inspections and
disclosed hundreds of open investigations in June, with new registration
legislation being requested. Knowing exactly where your scope begins and
ends has never mattered more than it does this year.

Which is the reason I built the August 29 seminar the way I did. It is not
about prescribing anything. It is about the nutritional protocol that
decides whether a patient thrives on a peptide or abandons it, and that is
squarely inside what you are licensed to recommend.
