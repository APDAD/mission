What They Found

One Hour Moved
The Charts.

 

Hope you are well. The clocks just handed everyone an hour back, and a BMJ cohort of 683,809 patients shows that single hour is measurable: the week after the autumn change, sleep-disorder diagnoses ran 8 percent lower, and anxiety diagnoses dipped too.

Sit with that for a second. One borrowed hour of sleep, one week, and the diagnosis rate moved. That is not a study about clocks, it is a study about how thin the average patient's sleep margin already is. The patients still lying awake this week are telling you something worth hearing.

On the support side, the newest meta-analysis of L-theanine, 18 randomized trials, found small but real improvements in sleep quality and how long it takes to fall asleep. Small is the honest word, the confidence intervals sit close to zero, and the outcomes are self-reported. It is a gentle tool for a wind-down routine, not a sedative, and that is exactly its use.
