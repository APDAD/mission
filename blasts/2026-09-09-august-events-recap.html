What August Looked Like

We Were In Three Rooms.
Here Is What Happened In Them.

 

Hope you are well. This one is not research. It is a look at where we actually were last month, because the best way to know whether a company is worth working with is to see whether it shows up.

Three events in seventeen days. A women's summit, a continuing education day for dietitians, and a four hour peptide seminar. Photos are below, and the practical takeaway from the first one is attached to this email.

Thursday, August 13 · Georgia Chiropractic Association

The Inaugural
GCA Women's Summit

 

The GCA ran its first ever Women's Summit and NutriDyn was a supporting sponsor. Dr. Deb Schreibman spoke on the integrated female practitioner, and later sat on the panel.

What made her talk land was not a product. It was a system. She showed the room how she screens a new female patient in the first few minutes of an intake, and what she does with each answer.

She also spoke openly about private labeling with NutriDyn, her own practice name on the bottle. She was direct about what it did for her. The practice took off, and the refill comes back to her instead of going to a shelf somewhere else. That was the part the room leaned in for.

One doctor in that room emailed us five days later and asked us to set her practice up. That is the whole reason we sponsor these.

Friday, August 28 · Harmony Nutrition

A Room Full Of
Registered Dietitians

 

Harmony Nutrition ran their first annual Continuing Education Day, and we spent it with a profession that does not usually get sold to well. Registered dietitians are careful, evidence first, and unimpressed by a pitch.

We answered questions about formulation, sourcing and what practitioner exclusive actually means, and we let the talks do the teaching. Shayna Komar, RD gave the integrative oncology session.

Three practitioners from that day started an account with us afterward. Not because a rep chased them, but because the answers held up.

Saturday, August 29 · Life University

Four Hours On Peptides,
Nothing Held Back

 

We brought Dr. Matt Chalmers in from Texas and gave him four hours in room 152 at Life University. Peptides, GLP-1 agonists, and the nutritional support that decides whether a patient tolerates any of it.

The honest version, which is why we ran it: most of what gets charted as a side effect is a nutrient gap wearing a costume. He covered how to apply peptides in an office, and just as usefully, how to support a patient well if you decide peptides are not for your practice.

One attendee sent a clinical question about a patient on a biologic that same weekend. Dr. Chalmers answered it directly. That is the level the room ran at.

If you could not be there, you still get the material. We put the seminar companion pages online and left them open. No login, no form. Read the peptide seminar companion pages.
