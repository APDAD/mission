The three waves

Bringing everything in at once is how good products sit unsold: nobody can explain them yet, and regulars find their spot changed overnight. Go in this order.

Wave 1 · now

One-for-one swaps

  • Same ingredient, same form, same dose: vitamin D3, fish oil, magnesium, CoQ10, BCAA.
  • The core powders whose match is already priced.
  • No study needed. The new bottle takes the old bottle's spot as it sells through.
Wave 2 · after training

Needs a story

  • Anything replacing a blend, not a single ingredient: sleep formulas, inflammation powders, multis.
  • Comes in only once its owner on staff has been trained (below).
  • Check the form before you swap: a packet is not a capsule, a one-a-day is not a three-a-day.
Wave 3 · with your programs

Paired at pickup

  • Pair the prescription with the nutrient it depletes, handed over at the counter.
  • For compounded and sterile scripts, a printed "this goes with this" stack card at pickup.
  • The pharmacist's recommendation does the selling. The card does the remembering.

The sticker system

Old habits reorder old products. A dot on the shelf tag stops that without anyone having to remember a list.

Red dot

  • Being discontinued. Whoever writes the order skips it.
  • It stays on the shelf and sells down normally.

Green dot

  • Its replacement is already chosen and on order.
  • Goes on the tag the day the order is placed.

The two-bottle rule

  • When a red-dot product is down to its last two bottles, the replacement goes on the very next order.
  • The new bottle lands before the last old one sells.
The gold line is the whole trick: order at two, and the new bottle is on the shelf before the old one is gone. Lead time and pace vary; the rule does not.

Two owners, two products, every cycle

Idea from the pharmacy floor, and the best one in the room: whoever has to present a product ends up owning it. Owners spot the selling moment at the counter because they know the bottle cold.

Pick two products

Start with the Wave 2 items you most want moving.

Pick two owners

One pharmacist and one tech works well. Rotate people every cycle.

Twenty minutes with Asher

Zoom or in person, at the time that suits them: who it is for, how to say it, what it pairs with.

They teach the huddle

Five minutes in the morning meeting. Everyone else learns it from a colleague.

Next cycle

Two new products, two new owners. In a few months every staff member owns something.

On the floor

Most shoppers turn right

  • The right-hand path from the main door is the hotspot.
  • Put the growing section there, not at the far end where shoppers arrive tired.

Regulars shop by spot and color

  • Many don't remember the brand, only where it sat and what it looked like.
  • Move one row at a time. Small tweaks land; big rearrangements get pushback.

Two or three brands, not seven

  • Keep a backup brand per category for backorders.
  • Past three, it is shelf space doing nothing.

Answer the question before it is asked

  • A short "why this brand" card on the shelf calms the shopper whose usual bottle moved.
