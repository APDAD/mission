Intestinal Permeability
Your gut barrier, and why it leaks

Your gut lining is a single layer of cells, sealed at the seams by tight junctions. It is built to let nutrients through and keep everything else out: undigested food, microbes and the toxins they make. Poor diet, infections, some medications, alcohol and chronic stress can loosen those seams. Researchers call this increased intestinal permeability; you may have heard it called leaky gut. The good news: your gut lining renews itself every few days, so the right food, habits and nutrients give it a real chance to rebuild.

Food reactionsBloating or symptoms after foods such as gluten, dairy or soy
Immune overloadSeasonal allergies, skin flare-ups, catching every cold
Poor absorptionRunning low on vitamin D, B12, iron, magnesium or zinc

Researchers are also studying how a leaky barrier connects to autoimmune conditions such as Hashimoto's thyroiditis, rheumatoid arthritis and lupus. If you live with one, ask your practitioner whether gut support belongs in your plan.

Lifestyle Modifications

Find and remove food triggers
  • Gluten
  • Dairy
  • Soy
  • Ask about a 3-week elimination trial
Protect your gut flora
  • Use antibiotics only when truly needed
  • Add one fermented food a day (kefir, yogurt, sauerkraut)
Eat whole, unprocessed foods
  • Cut added sugar and ultra-processed snacks
  • Fill half the plate with vegetables and fruit
  • Work up to 25 to 30 g of fiber a day
Ease the load on the lining
  • Keep alcohol low
  • Ask before regular ibuprofen or naproxen
  • Sleep 7 to 9 hours, manage stress
