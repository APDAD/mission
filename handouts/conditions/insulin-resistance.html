Understanding Insulin Resistance

Insulin is the hormone your pancreas makes to move sugar out of your blood and into your cells for fuel. In insulin resistance, the cells stop answering the signal well, so sugar lingers in the blood and the pancreas works harder to keep up. Years can pass this way, with normal-looking blood sugar, before it rises far enough to be called prediabetes or type 2 diabetes. Insulin resistance also feeds inflammation, high blood pressure, high cholesterol and fatty liver.

What drives it

  • Sugar and processed food
  • Sitting most of the day
  • Short or poor sleep
  • Chronic stress
  • Extra belly fat
  • Low magnesium or vitamin D
  • Family history

Signs you may notice

  • Sugar cravings
  • Tired after meals
  • Weight gain at the waist
  • Hungry soon after eating
  • Hard to lose weight
  • High triglycerides
  • Skin tags, dark skin patches

Where it leads

  • Type 2 diabetes
  • Heart disease and stroke
  • High blood pressure
  • Fatty liver (MASLD)
  • PCOS and hormone shifts
  • Memory decline
1 in 3U.S. adults has prediabetes, and more than 8 in 10 of them do not know it (CDC). A fasting insulin, glucose and A1c panel can catch insulin resistance years before diabetes.

Lifestyle-Based Medicine

Low-glycemic Mediterranean plate
  • Protein first at every meal
  • Half the plate vegetables
  • Whole-food carbs, paired with protein or fat
  • Three to five balanced meals
  • Limit sugar and sweet drinks
Lower stress, better sleep
  • Commit to a simple plan
  • Deep breathing, 5 minutes a day
  • 7 to 9 hours of sleep, same times daily
  • One 10-minute mindful walk a day
Move your muscles
  • Walk 10 minutes after meals
  • Strength training 2 to 3 times a week
  • Stand up every 30 to 60 minutes
  • Moderate to hard exercise 3 to 5 days a week
