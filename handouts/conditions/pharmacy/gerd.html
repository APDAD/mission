Acid Reflux and GERD
Gastroesophageal reflux disease

About 1 in 5 U.S. adults has GERD. At the bottom of your esophagus sits a ring of muscle, the lower esophageal sphincter (LES). It opens to let food into the stomach and closes to keep it there. In GERD the valve relaxes when it should not, so acid and digestive enzymes splash back into a tube that has no protective lining against them. Heartburn is the most common sign. Others include:

  • A sour taste or food coming back up
  • Hoarse voice, throat clearing
  • Chronic dry cough
  • Trouble swallowing
  • Bad breath
  • Fullness or upset stomach after meals
Call your doctor promptly if

food sticks when you swallow, you vomit blood or see black stools, you lose weight without trying, or you have chest pain (never assume it is only heartburn).

Left alone, reflux can inflame and scar the esophagus. A digestive tract that stays irritated also affects how well you absorb nutrients, and that ripples into energy, immunity and mood.

About acid-reducing medicines. Proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, esomeprazole, pantoprazole) and H2 blockers lower acid and help many people heal. With long-term use they are linked to low magnesium and vitamin B12, and the FDA has warned about low magnesium and bone fractures with long-term, high-dose PPI use.1 Do not stop one on your own: ask your prescriber and pharmacist for a plan. Food, habits and targeted nutrients can lower how often reflux shows up.

1. U.S. Food and Drug Administration. Drug Safety Communications on proton pump inhibitors: possible increased risk of fractures (2010) and low magnesium levels with long-term use (2011).

Lifestyle Modifications

Close the kitchen early

Finish eating 3 hours before you lie down.

Raise the head

Lift the head of your bed 6 to 8 inches.

Smaller meals

Eat slowly and stop before full.

A healthy weight

Even a few pounds lighter eases pressure on the valve.

Know your triggers

Fried food, chocolate, mint, caffeine, alcohol, citrus, tomato, fizzy drinks.

Stop smoking

Smoking relaxes the valve and slows healing.
