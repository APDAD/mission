Joint Pain and Osteoarthritis
Knees, hips and hands

Osteoarthritis (OA) is the most common kind of arthritis, and it changes the whole joint: the smooth cartilage on the ends of the bones thins and cracks, the bone underneath grows spurs, and the joint lining can swell. It shows up most in knees, hips and hands and affects about 528 million people worldwide. Moving more, carrying less weight and the right nutrients help many people stay active.

How it can feel
  • Pain when you use the joint, easing with rest
  • Stiff on waking or after sitting, loose within 30 minutes
  • Creaking, grinding or clicking as it bends
  • Some swelling, or a joint that feels unsteady
  • Stairs, kneeling or opening jars get harder
  • Hard knobs on the finger joints
Get checked promptly if
  • A hot, red, very swollen joint, worse with fever: go today
  • Morning stiffness over 30 minutes, or many joints at once
  • It began with a fall or injury, or is worsening fast
  • The joint locks, gives way or changes shape

Lifestyle RecommendationsCartilage has no blood supply. Moving is how it gets fed.

Move most days

150 minutes a week of walking, cycling or swimming. Short bouts count.

Build strength

Thigh and hip work 2 days a week takes load off the joint.

Lighten the load

Each pound lost takes about 4 pounds off your knees with every step.

Tai chi or yoga

Slow, steady moves ease stiffness and build balance.

Eat to calm

Fish, olive oil, beans, nuts and colorful plants. Fewer sugary drinks.

Heat and support

Warmth for stiffness, ice after a flare, a cane in the other hand.

Pharmacist check

  • On a blood thinner? Fish oil, curcumin, ginger, willow and glucosamine can add to bleeding: ask your pharmacist first.
  • Willow works like mild aspirin: skip it with an aspirin allergy; do not stack it with aspirin, ibuprofen or naproxen.
