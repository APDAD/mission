Better Sleep
When falling asleep or staying asleep is hard

Adults need 7 or more hours of sleep, yet more than 1 in 3 U.S. adults usually get less (CDC). Insomnia means trouble falling asleep, staying asleep, or waking too early, even with time to sleep. Sleep runs on a body clock in your brain: light sets it each morning, and the hormone melatonin tells your body that night has come.

Is it long-term insomnia?

  • Trouble falling or staying asleep, or waking too early
  • 3 or more nights a week, for 3+ months
  • Your days suffer: tired, moody or foggy

All 3? Ask about CBT-I, the sleep-coaching program experts recommend first.

Sleep apnea warning signs

  • Loud snoring
  • Sleepy or tired by day
  • Gasping or breathing pauses in sleep
  • High blood pressure
  • Large collar size

Snoring or breathing pauses, plus one more box? Ask about a sleep test (a doctor orders it).

Sources: CDC; AASM (ICSD-3-TR; sleep apnea guideline 2009); American College of Physicians 2016.

Reset Your Sleep ClockHabits from CBT-I, the sleep-coaching program experts recommend first

Morning: set the clock
  • Get up at the same time every day, even after a rough night
  • Get outdoor light within an hour of waking
  • Once you wake, get up: your bed is for sleep
Day: build sleep drive
  • Sleep drive builds while you are awake; naps drain it
  • Skip naps, or keep them short and early
  • Caffeine in the morning only
  • Move your body most days
Night: wind down
  • Dim lights and put screens away an hour before bed
  • No alcohol or big meals late
  • A cool, dark, quiet bedroom
  • Awake 20 minutes? Get up. Go back to bed when sleepy

Pharmacist checkKeep taking your medicines.

  • Ask your pharmacist before GoodNight if you take antidepressants (SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs), migraine triptans or sleep pills: it adds to them. Do not drive after a dose. Its B6 (up to 40 mg) adds up: tingling hands or feet? Stop it and ask.
  • Snoring or gasping? Go over the apnea check on page 1 with your pharmacist. A doctor orders the sleep test; supplements are no substitute for apnea care.
  • Pick Mag Calm Pro (200 mg magnesium) or Magnesium Glycinate (260 mg), not both: either stays under the 350 mg daily limit for supplements. Kidney disease or an antibiotic? Ask first. Keep it 4+ hours from thyroid or bone pills.
  • Pregnant, nursing or on medicine? Ask your pharmacist first. No Cortisol Pro if pregnant. Its ashwagandha can raise thyroid levels and add to sleep pills. Yellow skin or eyes? Stop it and call a doctor.
