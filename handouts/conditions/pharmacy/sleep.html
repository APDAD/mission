Better Sleep
When falling asleep or staying asleep is hard

Adults need 7 or more hours of sleep, yet more than 1 in 3 U.S. adults usually get less (CDC). Insomnia means trouble falling asleep, staying asleep, or waking too early, even with time to sleep. Sleep runs on a body clock in your brain: light sets it each morning, and the hormone melatonin tells your body that night has come.

Is it insomnia?

  • Trouble falling or staying asleep, or waking too early
  • 3 or more nights a week, for 3+ months
  • Your days suffer: tired, moody or foggy

All 3? Ask about CBT-I, the short sleep-coaching program experts recommend first.

Sleep apnea check (STOP-Bang)

  • SSnore loudly
  • TTired or sleepy by day
  • OSeen to stop breathing or gasp
  • PHigh blood pressure, or pills for it
  • BBMI over 35
  • AAge over 50
  • NCollar 17 in+ (men), 16 in+ (women)
  • GMale

3 or more ticks? Ask your pharmacist about a sleep apnea test.

Sources: CDC; American Academy of Sleep Medicine (ICSD-3); STOP-Bang, Chung 2016; American College of Physicians 2016.

Reset Your Sleep ClockHabits from CBT-I, the first treatment sleep experts recommend for long-term insomnia

Morning: set the clock
  • Get up at the same time every day, weekends too
  • Get outdoor light within an hour of waking
  • Rise on time, even after a rough night
Day: build sleep drive
  • Have caffeine in the morning only
  • Move your body most days
  • Skip naps, or keep them short and early
Night: wind down
  • Dim lights and put screens away an hour before bed
  • No alcohol or big meals late in the evening
  • Keep the bedroom cool, dark and quiet
  • Awake 20 minutes? Get up until you feel sleepy
