Inflammatory Bowel Disease
Ulcerative colitis and Crohn's disease

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is a long-term condition in which the immune system keeps part of the digestive tract inflamed. More than 2 million Americans live with it. The two main types, ulcerative colitis and Crohn's disease, share belly pain, urgent diarrhea, tiredness and weight loss, with flares that come and go between quieter stretches. A gastroenterologist leads the care. Food, lifestyle and nutrient support work alongside that care, never instead of it.

Lifestyle Modifications

Find food triggers

Remove foods your care team identifies as a sensitivity or intolerance.

Limit sugar

Sugar and ultra-processed food feed the wrong microbes.

Eat enough

More protein and calories during flares, to protect weight and muscle.

Lower stress

Stress is one of the most common flare triggers.

Ask about NSAIDs

Ibuprofen and naproxen can trigger flares. Use antibiotics only when needed.

Move gently

Regular low to moderate exercise.

Pharmacist check

  • Steroids (prednisone, budesonide) lower vitamin D and calcium. Ask about bone health.
  • Sulfasalazine and methotrexate lower folate. Ask whether you need a folate supplement.
  • Never stop IBD medicine during a good stretch without your gastroenterologist. Ask before any new supplement.
