Polycystic Ovary Syndrome
PCOS

PCOS is a hormone condition in which the ovaries make more androgens (male-type hormones that women normally make in small amounts) than usual. It can disrupt periods, ovulation and fertility, and it shows up in skin, hair, weight and mood. Genes play a part, but insulin resistance is the engine behind most cases, which is why food, movement, sleep and stress change how PCOS behaves.

PCOS fact

Up to 1 in 8 women of reproductive age has PCOS, and up to 70 percent have never been diagnosed (World Health Organization). PCOS happens at every body size: you can have it at a normal weight.

Risk factors

  • You can change: chronic stress, a high-sugar, low-nutrient diet, inactivity and weight swings, hormone-disrupting chemicals
  • Genetic: a mother or sister with PCOS or type 2 diabetes raises your risk

Lifestyle Recommendations

Diet

Protein and fiber at every meal. Limit refined sugar and starch to steady blood sugar.

Exercise

150 minutes a week of activity plus 2 strength days improves insulin sensitivity.

Sleep

Seven to eight hours a night supports weight and healthy hormone production.

Limit hormone disruptors

Glass or steel containers, never heat food in plastic, fragrance-free products.
