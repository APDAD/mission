Intestinal Permeability
Your gut barrier, and why it leaks

Your gut lining is a wall just one cell thick, sealed at the seams by tight junctions. A healthy lining lets nutrients through and keeps out undigested food, microbes and the toxins they make. Poor diet, infections, alcohol, some medications and long-term stress can loosen those seams. Researchers call this increased intestinal permeability; you may know it as leaky gut. The good news: the lining renews itself every few days, so the right food, habits and nutrients help it rebuild.

When the barrier leaks, you may notice

Food reactionsBloating or other symptoms after foods such as gluten, dairy or soy

Immune overloadSeasonal allergies, skin flare-ups, catching every cold

Poor absorptionRunning low on vitamin D, B12, iron, magnesium or zinc

Researchers are also studying how a leaky barrier connects to autoimmune conditions such as Hashimoto's thyroiditis, rheumatoid arthritis and lupus. If you live with one, ask your pharmacist whether gut support belongs in your plan.

Lifestyle Modifications

Find and remove food triggers
  • Gluten
  • Dairy
  • Soy
  • Ask about a 3-week elimination trial
Protect your gut flora
  • Use antibiotics only when truly needed
  • Add one fermented food a day (kefir, yogurt, sauerkraut)
Eat whole, unprocessed foods
  • Cut added sugar and ultra-processed snacks
  • Fill half the plate with vegetables and fruit
  • Work up to 25 to 30 g of fiber a day
Ease the load on the lining
  • Keep alcohol low
  • Ask before regular ibuprofen or naproxen
  • Sleep 7 to 9 hours, manage stress
