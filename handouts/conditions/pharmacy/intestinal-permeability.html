Intestinal Permeability
Your gut barrier, and why it leaks

Your gut lining is a wall just one cell thick, sealed at the seams by tight junctions. A healthy lining lets nutrients through and keeps out undigested food, microbes and the toxins they make. Poor diet, infections, alcohol, some medications and stress can loosen those seams. Researchers call this increased intestinal permeability; you may know it as leaky gut. The good news: the lining renews itself every few days, and food and daily habits help it stay strong.

When the barrier leaks, you may notice

Food reactionsBloating or other symptoms after foods such as gluten, dairy or soy

Immune reactionsAllergies and flare-ups of eczema (itchy, red skin)

Poor absorptionDamaged cells absorb less iron, zinc or vitamin D, so you may tire

Researchers are also studying how a leaky barrier connects to autoimmune conditions such as Hashimoto’s thyroiditis, rheumatoid arthritis and lupus. If you live with one, tell your pharmacist and your doctor about any gut symptoms.

Lifestyle ModificationsFrom NutriDyn’s 5R gut plan: Replace, Repair, Reinoculate and Retain here; Remove’s herbs are on the Gut Overgrowth sheet.

Days 1 to 10: take a break from triggers
  • Gluten, dairy and soy
  • Sugar, artificial sweeteners and alcohol
  • Ultra-processed food and drinks
Eat to feed the lining
  • 5 or more fruits and vegetables a day, 1 leafy green
  • Good-quality protein and healthy fats
  • 1 fermented food a day: sauerkraut, kimchi or kombucha
Days 11 to 24: bring foods back one at a time
  • Gluten, then dairy, then sugar: 1 serving a day for 3 days
  • Keep alcohol, coffee and spicy food out
  • A reaction? Stop that food and tell your care team
Ease the load on the lining
  • Ask before regular ibuprofen or naproxen
  • Eat slowly and calmly; nothing 2 hours before bed
  • Move 30 minutes, 5 days a week; 10 calm minutes daily

Pharmacist checkKeep taking your medicines.

  • Ulcer, now or ever? Skip Digestive Complete. Ask first with gastritis, acid blockers, NSAIDs or steroids.
  • Zinc, iron and calcium in the shakes bind quinolone and tetracycline antibiotics: take those 2 hours before or 6 after.
  • Probiotic: antifungals stop its S. boulardii; not with a central IV line or weak immunity.
  • Pregnant or nursing? Skip GI Integrity; ask before the others. Celiac test? Do it before you stop gluten.
  • Blood thinner? Ask first (glucosamine, turmeric). Alpha-gal? Skip Digestive Complete and GI Defend (pork, beef). Smoker or ex-smoker? Ask before GI Restore (high in beta-carotene).
