Healing After Injury
Sprains, strains, whiplash and recovery from surgery

A sprain hurts a ligament, which ties bone to bone. A strain hurts a muscle or the tendon that ties it to bone. After a sprain, strain, whiplash or surgery, the body heals in three overlapping phases. Inflammation comes first, and it is part of healing. Then repair cells lay down new collagen, the rope-like protein of ligaments and tendons. Over months, the patch is remodeled and grows stronger. How you eat, sleep and move helps each phase.

Get care right awayHealing should get a little better week by week. Some signs cannot wait.

Call 911 now

  • Chest pain or sudden trouble breathing
  • Stroke signs: face drooping, a weak or numb arm or leg, trouble speaking or seeing, severe dizziness, or a sudden, severe headache or neck pain
  • After a crash or blow to the head: passing out, a seizure, a worsening headache, repeated vomiting, confusion or unusual sleepiness
  • New weakness or numbness with loss of bladder or bowel control

Call your doctor the same day

  • Calf pain or swelling in one leg
  • Fever, or redness, warmth or cloudy drainage at a surgery cut
  • Numbness or tingling that builds slowly
  • Pain or swelling getting worse instead of better, or you cannot put weight on it

Lifestyle Recommendations

Protein every meal

Fish, eggs, chicken or beans, a palm-size portion.

Vitamin C every day

Oranges, kiwi and peppers help you make collagen.

Sleep 7 to 9 hours

Short sleep slows repair. Keep a steady bedtime.

Move as advised

Gentle motion early, then exercises that line fibers up.

Skip smoking

Smoking starves tissue of oxygen. Go easy on alcohol.

Pharmacist checkKeep taking your medicines.

  • Bleeding problem or blood thinner (warfarin, apixaban, clopidogrel), aspirin, or NSAIDs like ibuprofen or naproxen? White willow (salicin), ginger, curcumin, devil's claw and fish oil may add to bleeding risk. Ask your pharmacist and your prescriber.
  • Allergic to aspirin? Skip Inflam-Eze Plus: white willow contains salicin, a natural relative of aspirin.
  • Surgery coming up, or just had one? Tell your surgeon about every supplement and ask when to stop and restart. Many ask you to stop herbs and fish oil 2 weeks before surgery.
  • Muscle relaxers, opioids or sleep aids? Muscle Eze and the hops in Inflam-Eze Plus may add drowsiness. Diabetes medicine? The ginger and curcumin in Inflam-Eze Plus may lower blood sugar; ask your pharmacist about checking it.
  • Antibiotics or thyroid medicine? Calcium and magnesium in Muscle Eze can block some of them; ask your pharmacist how to space them.
  • Pregnant, nursing, under 18, allergic to fish, shellfish or milk, or have liver or kidney disease, stomach ulcers or gallstones? Ask your pharmacist first.
