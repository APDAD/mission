Thyroid Health
Underactive thyroid and Hashimoto’s

Your thyroid, a butterfly-shaped gland in your neck, sets the pace of nearly every cell. Too little hormone slows the body down: an underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism). In the U.S. the usual cause is Hashimoto’s, an immune attack on the gland.

Higher risk: women (5 to 8 times more likely than men), age 60 or older, a family history, type 1 diabetes, celiac or another autoimmune disease, a baby in the past 6 months, thyroid surgery, radioactive iodine, radiation to the neck or chest, and some medicines (lithium, amiodarone).

Lifestyle Recommendations

Whole-food plate

Fish, eggs, beans, nuts and seeds bring selenium, zinc and iron.

Go easy on iodine

No kelp or iodine drops. Iodized salt or a multi (150 mcg) is plenty.

Move most days

Walk most days. Add strength work 2 to 3 days a week.

Sleep 7 to 9 hours

Steady sleep helps energy, mood and weight.

Ask about celiac

More common with thyroid disease. Test before you cut gluten.

Same time daily

Take thyroid medicine the same way each day, with water only.

Pharmacist checkKeep taking your medicines.

  • Thyroid medicine alone: water, empty stomach, 30 to 60 minutes before breakfast. Iron, calcium, magnesium, antacids and multis 4 hours later. Bedtime dose? Ask your pharmacist to re-time them.
  • Biotin can throw off thyroid labs. It is in many multis (Essential Multi too) and hair, skin and nail formulas. Stop it 2 to 3 days before a blood test.
  • D3 5000 and Iron Support need a low lab first. With the multi, D3 is 6,000 IU a day, over the 4,000 IU upper limit: recheck it.
  • On warfarin or another blood thinner? Skip D3 5000 with K2 (its label says so); ask your pharmacist about plain D3 5000 and the multi’s vitamin K.
  • Never change your thyroid dose on your own. Glandular thyroid products can hold real hormone. Pregnant or planning? Tell your prescriber right away: the dose usually goes up, and iodine needs rise.
