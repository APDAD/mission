Gut Overgrowth
Yeast, parasites and SIBO

Your gut is home to trillions of microbes, and almost all of them live in the colon (large intestine). Stomach acid, the gut’s sweeping waves and your immune system keep the small intestine nearly free of bacteria and keep yeast and parasites in check. When those defenses slip, microbes can overgrow: bacteria crowd the small intestine (SIBO), Candida yeast multiplies, or a parasite moves in. A test ordered by your provider shows which one is behind your symptoms.

Lifestyle RecommendationsStarve it, sweep it, and keep new invaders out

Cut the sugar

Skip added sugar, sweet drinks, alcohol and junk food during the plan.

Space your meals

No grazing: the gut’s cleaning waves run between meals. Stop eating 2 hours before bed.

Wash, cook, filter

Wash hands and produce, cook meat through, and skip untreated lake or stream water.

Eat calm, chew well

Slow, unhurried meals give stomach acid and enzymes a head start.

Review acid blockers

They lower stomach acid, a key defense. Never stop one without your prescriber.

Pharmacist check

  • Berberine (Spectrum BR) slows CYP3A4, 2D6 and 2C9 and adds to diabetes medicines. Bring your full medicine list.
  • Blood thinners: garlic and pau d’arco may add to bleeding, and chlorella (in Binder Pro) has vitamin K, which can weaken warfarin.
  • Skip the herbs in pregnancy or nursing. Skip S. boulardii, a live yeast, with a central IV line or weak immunity; antifungals may stop it working.
