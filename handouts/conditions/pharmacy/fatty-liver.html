Fatty Liver Disease
MASLD, the condition formerly called NAFLD

Fatty liver disease means extra fat is stored inside liver cells. In 2023, liver specialists named the most common kind MASLD (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease) because it goes hand in hand with insulin resistance, belly fat, high triglycerides and high blood pressure. This kind is not caused by heavy drinking. It is the most common liver condition in the world, affecting about 1 in 3 adults, and most people feel nothing. Caught early, it is often reversible.

Ask about liver checksYou may feel fine. Ask your pharmacist which checks fit you; your doctor can order them.
1Liver enzymes (ALT, AST)

A routine blood test. High levels can mean the liver is irritated, but normal levels do not rule out fatty liver.

  • Healthy ALT, women: up to 25 U/L
  • Healthy ALT, men: up to 33 U/L
2FIB-4 score

Worked out from your age and routine labs (ALT, AST, platelets) to estimate the risk of advanced scarring.

  • Under 1.3: low risk
  • 1.3 or higher: a FibroScan is next
3FibroScan (elastography)

A no-needle scan that measures liver stiffness (scarring) and estimates liver fat. The next step if FIB-4 is 1.3 or higher.

  • Under 8 kPa: low risk
  • 8 kPa or more: see a liver specialist
Two or more metabolic risks, like prediabetes or belly fat? Check FIB-4 every 1 to 2 years.AASLD practice guidance, 2023

Lifestyle Recommendations

Eat Mediterranean

Vegetables, beans, olive oil, nuts and fish. Skip sugary drinks and sweets: sugar easily turns into liver fat.

Lose extra weight

Extra weight? Losing 5% cuts liver fat; 7–10% calms inflammation; 10% or more eases scarring.

Move every day

150 minutes a week of brisk walking plus 2 strength days. A 10-minute walk after meals blunts the sugar spike.

Feed your gut

Fiber and colorful plants feed helpful gut bacteria, like Akkermansia, that help keep the gut lining strong.

Less alcohol, or none

Even if alcohol did not cause this, it adds to the liver’s load. Less is better; none at all if there is scarring.

Pharmacist checkKeep taking your medicines.

  • Diabetes medicines: Berberine Pro (and, more mildly, the alpha-lipoic acid in Cellular Energy) may lower blood sugar along with them. Ask your doctor before you start, and check your blood sugar more often.
  • Statins, warfarin and other medicines: Berberine Pro can slow the enzymes that clear some drugs (CYP2D6, 2C9, 3A4), including warfarin, simvastatin, lovastatin and atorvastatin, and the CoQ10 in Cellular Energy may change how warfarin works. Show your pharmacist your full medicine list; your doctor may recheck your INR (a clotting test) after you start.
  • Pregnant, planning to be, or nursing? Ask your pharmacist before taking any of these, and skip Berberine Pro unless your doctor says otherwise.
  • Gallstones, a blocked bile duct, or a ragweed or daisy allergy? Ask your pharmacist or your doctor before Lipo-Flow (it boosts bile flow) and Milk Thistle Complex.
