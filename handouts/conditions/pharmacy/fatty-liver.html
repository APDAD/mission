Fatty Liver Disease
MASLD, the condition formerly called NAFLD

Fatty liver disease means extra fat is stored inside liver cells, and it is not caused by heavy drinking. In 2023, liver specialists renamed it MASLD (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease) because it goes hand in hand with insulin resistance, belly fat, high triglycerides and high blood pressure. It is the most common liver condition in the world, affecting about 1 in 3 adults, and most people feel nothing. Caught early, it is often reversible.

Ask about these liver checksFatty liver is usually silent. Ask your pharmacist which checks fit you.
1Liver enzymes (ALT, AST)

A routine blood test. High levels can mean the liver is irritated, but normal levels do not rule out fatty liver. Many labs’ “normal” ranges run higher than ideal.

2FIB-4 score

Worked out from your age and routine labs (ALT, AST, platelets) to estimate the risk of advanced scarring.

  • Under 1.3: low risk
  • 1.3 or higher: step 3 is next
3FibroScan (elastography)

A no-needle scan that measures liver stiffness (scarring) and liver fat. Often the next step when FIB-4 is 1.3 or higher.

  • Under 8 kPa: low risk
  • 8 kPa or more: see a liver specialist
Two or more metabolic risks, like prediabetes or belly fat? Repeat FIB-4 every 1 to 2 years.AASLD practice guidance, 2023

Lifestyle Recommendations

Eat Mediterranean

Vegetables, lean protein, olive oil, nuts and fish. Cut sugary drinks, juice and refined carbs: they turn into liver fat fastest.

Lose 7 to 10 percent

Losing 7 to 10 percent of body weight is the change most tied to clearing liver fat and calming inflammation.

Move every day

150 minutes a week of brisk walking plus 2 strength days. A 10-minute walk after meals blunts the sugar spike.

Go easy on alcohol

Even though alcohol did not cause it, alcohol adds to the liver's load. Keep it minimal or skip it.
