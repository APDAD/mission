Menopause and Perimenopause
Every system changes. Every system can be supported.

Menopause is the day you reach 12 months in a row with no period, on average around age 51. The years before it, perimenopause, often last 4 to 8 years while estrogen and progesterone swing, then fall. These hormones act on nearly every tissue, so changes show up from head to toe. Hormone therapy is the most effective treatment for hot flashes: ask your pharmacist if it fits you.

Hot flashes: what works best

  1. Hormone therapy works best. For most healthy women under 60, the benefits outweigh the risks. Ask your pharmacist.
  2. Also proven: CBT, clinical hypnosis and some prescriptions.
  3. Supplements are not recommended for hot flashes. EstroG‑100, in Menopause Support, helped in small trials; the newest was paid for by a supplement company.
Source: The Menopause Society, 2022 and 2023

Lifestyle Recommendations

Lift weights

Strength work 2 to 3 days a week guards muscle and bone.

Walk most days

150 minutes a week helps your heart, mood, sleep and weight.

Bone-smart plate

Protein at each meal and calcium foods like yogurt and greens.

Guard your sleep

Same bedtime, a cool dark room, less alcohol and late caffeine.

Try CBT

CBT and clinical hypnosis are proven to ease hot flashes.

Skip smoking

Smoking speeds bone loss and raises heart risk.

Pharmacist check

  • Had breast cancer or another estrogen-dependent cancer? The label says not to use Menopause Support. With black cohosh, stop if urine darkens or skin yellows.
  • Warfarin? The vitamin K in D3 5000 with K2 can change your INR. Thyroid, sedative or diabetes medicine? Ashwagandha can add to it.
  • Space magnesium 2 hours from bisphosphonates and some antibiotics. Creatine can raise the creatinine number on kidney labs.
