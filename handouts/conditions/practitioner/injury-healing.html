Healing After Injury
Sprains, strains, whiplash and recovery from surgery

After a sprain, strain, whiplash or surgery, your body repairs the hurt tissue in three overlapping phases. Inflammation comes first, and it is part of healing. Next, repair cells lay down new collagen, the rope-like protein that ligaments and tendons are made of. Then, over months, the patch is remodeled and grows stronger. How you eat, sleep and move between visits helps each phase do its job.

Get care right awayHealing should get a little better week by week. Some signs cannot wait.

Call 911 now

  • Chest pain or sudden trouble breathing
  • After a crash or blow to the head: a headache that keeps getting worse, repeated vomiting, slurred speech, confusion or unusual sleepiness
  • New weakness or numbness with loss of bladder or bowel control

Call your doctor the same day

  • Calf pain or swelling in one leg
  • Fever, or redness, warmth or cloudy drainage at a surgery cut
  • New numbness or tingling in an arm or leg
  • Pain or swelling getting worse instead of better, or you cannot put weight on it

Lifestyle Recommendations

Protein every meal

A palm-size portion of fish, eggs, chicken or beans. Repair runs on protein.

Color for vitamin C

Citrus, kiwi, berries and peppers. Your body needs vitamin C to build collagen.

Sleep 7 to 9 hours

Short sleep slows repair. Keep a steady bedtime, even on weekends.

Move as advised

Gentle motion early, then your exercises. Load lines the new fibers up.

Skip smoking

Smoking starves healing tissue of oxygen. Keep alcohol low, too.
