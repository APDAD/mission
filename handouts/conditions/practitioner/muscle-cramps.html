Muscle Cramps and Spasms
Why a muscle locks up, and how to help it let go

A cramp, or spasm, is a muscle that suddenly tightens on its own and will not let go. It usually grabs the calf, foot or thigh, often at night. Cramps are very common: up to 6 in 10 adults have had a night leg cramp, and nearly half of people over 60 get them. Most are harmless, and simple habits help. Here is what happens inside the muscle.

1

The nerve says “squeeze.” Sodium and potassium carry the signal into the muscle.

2

Calcium switches it ON. Thin and thick filaments grab and slide, so the muscle shortens.

3

Magnesium and ATP switch it OFF. The filaments let go and calcium goes back into storage.

4

A cramp is the switch stuck ON. A tired or over-excited nerve keeps firing.

What the research says
Helped

Stretching at bedtime. Adults over 55 who stretched calves and hamstrings nightly for 6 weeks had fewer, milder night cramps.

Promising

Vitamin K2 (MK-7). Adults 65+ taking 180 mcg nightly for 8 weeks went from 2.6 cramps a week to about 1; the placebo group rose to 3.6. One trial so far.

Mixed

Magnesium. Unlikely to prevent ordinary night cramps in older adults (Cochrane review). A small trial tied it to less soreness after hard exercise.

Mixed

CoQ10 with a statin. One review of trials found less muscle pain and cramping; another found no clear benefit.

Hallegraeff 2012 · Tan 2024 · Cochrane 2020 · Reno 2022 · Qu 2018 · Banach 2015

Get checked
  • A sore calf that is swollen, warm or red. It can be a blood clot. Get care today.
  • On a statin, with muscle pain or weakness you can’t explain, or dark urine. Call your prescriber right away.
  • Cramps that come often, wake you most nights, or come with weakness or numbness. Have a doctor check them.

Quinine pills are no longer advised for leg cramps.

Lifestyle Recommendations

Stretch before bed

Gently stretch your calves and the backs of your thighs every night before sleep.

Drink, plus salts

Sip water all day. After long, sweaty workouts, add electrolytes, not just water.

Eat your minerals

Greens, beans, nuts and seeds for magnesium. Potatoes and bananas for potassium.

Build up slowly

Add exercise a little at a time. Tired muscles cramp more easily.

Stop a cramp

Straighten the leg, pull your toes toward you, rub the knot, add warmth.

Check medicines

Water pills, statins and some inhalers can play a part. Ask first.

Provider check

  • Bring your medicine list. Water pills, statins, some asthma inhalers and some bone or estrogen medicines are linked to cramps. Never stop one on your own.
  • Warfarin (Coumadin)? Vitamin K2 and CoQ10 can change how it works. Start neither without your prescriber.
  • Kidney disease, spironolactone, an ACE inhibitor or an ARB? Ask your provider before Mag-K or extra magnesium. Loose stools mean too much magnesium.
