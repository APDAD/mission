Mold Illness
Mycotoxin exposure and recovery

Mold grows wherever water lingers: a slow roof leak, a damp crawl space, a sweating HVAC line. Some molds release mycotoxins and other irritants into the air you breathe. Damp, moldy buildings are linked with coughing, wheezing and asthma flares, and many people also report fatigue, headaches, joint aches and brain fog. Because the symptoms are so broad, mold is easy to overlook. Recovery starts with removing the exposure; only then does supporting the body's clearance make sense.

Lifestyle Recommendations

Limit moldy foods

Peanuts, corn, long-stored grains, dried fruit, wine and aged cheese carry more mycotoxins.

More fiber

Fiber binds toxins carried in bile. Aim for 30 g a day.

Dry the air

Keep indoor humidity between 30 and 50 percent with a dehumidifier.

Filter it

HEPA air purifiers and HEPA vacuums trap mold spores.

Sweat

Sauna or a good workout helps you sweat. Drink water.

Fix fast

Dry or repair any leak within 24 to 48 hours.
