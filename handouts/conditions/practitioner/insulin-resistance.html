Understanding Insulin Resistance

Insulin is a hormone your pancreas releases, especially after you eat. It is a messenger: it tells your cells to open their glucose doors, so sugar (glucose) can leave your blood and go into the cells for fuel. With insulin resistance, the cells stop listening well. Sugar backs up in the blood, so the pancreas makes more and more insulin to keep up. This can go on quietly for years before blood sugar rises high enough to be called prediabetes or type 2 diabetes.

What drives it

  • Sugary drinks and processed food
  • Sitting most of the day
  • Short sleep, long-term stress
  • Extra fat around the belly
  • Family history, age 45 or older
  • Some medicines, such as steroids

Clues to watch for

  • Often no signs at all
  • Weight gain at the waist
  • Tired or foggy after meals
  • Cravings, hungry soon after eating
  • Dark velvety skin patches, skin tags
  • High triglycerides, low HDL

Where it can lead

  • Prediabetes, then type 2 diabetes
  • Heart disease and stroke
  • High blood pressure
  • Fatty liver (MASLD)
  • PCOS and hormone shifts
  • Memory decline
More than1 in 3

U.S. adults have prediabetes, and more than 8 in 10 of them do not know it (CDC). Insulin often climbs years before blood sugar does, so ask your provider about a fasting insulin test along with the numbers below.

Lifestyle-Based Medicine

Low-glycemic Mediterranean plate
  • Protein first at every meal
  • Half the plate vegetables
  • Whole-food carbs, paired with protein or fat
  • Three to five balanced meals
  • Limit sugar and sweet drinks
Lower stress, better sleep
  • Commit to a simple plan
  • Deep breathing, 5 minutes a day
  • 7 to 9 hours of sleep, same times daily
  • One 10-minute mindful walk a day
Move your muscles
  • Walk 10 minutes after meals
  • Strength training 2 to 3 times a week
  • Stand up every 30 to 60 minutes
  • Moderate to hard exercise 3 to 5 days a week
