Lyme Disease
and other tickborne infections

Lyme disease is the most common illness spread by ticks in the U.S. An estimated 476,000 Americans are diagnosed and treated for it each year. It is caused by Borrelia bacteria passed on by the bite of an infected blacklegged (deer) tick, and the same ticks can carry other infections at the same time. The best intervention is prevention, and the second best is early treatment.

HOW IT SPREADS
SYMPTOMS
EARLY: DAYS TO WEEKSLATER: WEEKS TO MONTHS
See a clinician promptly if a rash, fever, aches or joint swelling show up after a tick bite or time outdoors. Early antibiotic treatment works best.
Not everyone sees the tick. Nymphs are the size of a poppy seed, and many people never get the bull's-eye rash, which is why Lyme gets mistaken for flu, fibromyalgia or chronic fatigue.
After treatment, some people have fatigue, pain or brain fog that lingers for months. That recovery stretch is where food, sleep and the support on the back of this page come in.
KNOW YOUR TICKS
Blacklegged (deer) tickEastern U.S., Georgia included. The main Lyme carrier.
Western blackleggedPacific coast states. Also spreads Lyme.
Lone star tickCommon across the Southeast. Ehrlichiosis, alpha-gal allergy.

Lifestyle RecommendationsAvoiding tick bites

Clothing

Light colors, long sleeves, pants tucked into socks. Treat gear with permethrin.

Repellent

Use an EPA-registered repellent: DEET, picaridin, IR3535 or oil of lemon eucalyptus.

High heat

Tumble dry clothes on high for 10 minutes after outdoor time to kill ticks.

Shower and check

Shower within 2 hours. Check scalp, ears, armpits, waist and behind knees.

Pets

Check pets daily and ask your vet about tick prevention.

Remove it right

Fine-tipped tweezers, close to the skin, pull straight up. Note the date.
