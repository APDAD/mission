Lyme Disease
and other tickborne infections

Lyme disease is the most common illness spread by ticks in the U.S.: an estimated 476,000 Americans are diagnosed and treated for it each year. Borrelia bacteria pass from the bite of an infected blacklegged (deer) tick, and the same tick can carry other germs too. Avoiding bites is the best defense; early treatment is the next best.

HOW IT SPREADS

SYMPTOMS

See a clinician promptly if a rash, fever, aches or swollen joints follow a tick bite or time outdoors. Early antibiotics work best.
Not everyone sees the tick. Nymphs are poppy-seed small. Up to 3 in 10 people never get a rash, and many rashes are plain red, not a bull's-eye. Early Lyme can pass for the flu.
After treatment, some people have tiredness, pain or brain fog that lingers for months. That recovery stretch is where food, sleep and the support on the back of this page come in.

KNOW YOUR TICKS adult females shown

Blacklegged (deer) tickBlack legs and shield, red-orange body. Eastern U.S., Georgia included. The main Lyme carrier.
Western blacklegged tickPacific coast states. A darker, red-brown cousin of the deer tick. It also spreads Lyme.
Lone star tickWhite dot on the female's back. Spreads ehrlichiosis and can trigger alpha-gal (red-meat) allergy.

Lifestyle RecommendationsAvoiding tick bites

Clothing

Light colors, long sleeves, pants tucked into socks. Treat gear with permethrin.

Repellent

Use an EPA-registered repellent: DEET, picaridin, IR3535 or oil of lemon eucalyptus.

High heat

Tumble dry clothes on high for 10 minutes after outdoor time to kill ticks.

Shower and check

Shower within 2 hours. Check scalp, ears, armpits, waist and behind knees.

Pets

Check pets daily and ask your vet about tick prevention.

Remove it right

Fine-tipped tweezers, close to the skin, pull straight up. Note the date.
