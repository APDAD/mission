Inflammatory Bowel Disease
Ulcerative colitis and Crohn’s disease

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is a long-term condition in which the immune system keeps part of the digestive tract inflamed. More than 2 million Americans live with it. The two main types, ulcerative colitis and Crohn’s disease, share belly pain, urgent diarrhea, tiredness and weight loss, with flares that come and go between quieter stretches. A gastroenterologist leads the care. Food, lifestyle and nutrient support work alongside that care, never instead of it.

Lifestyle ModificationsKeep every prescribed IBD medicine; your gastroenterologist leads. These steps and nutrients work alongside that care.

Mediterranean

Fish, olive oil and lots of vegetables; less sugar and processed food.

Spot triggers

Skip gluten, dairy and soy for 10 days, then add back one at a time.

Flare days

Small meals with protein. Cook or blend produce if raw hurts.

Lower stress

10 calm minutes and a gentle walk daily. Stress can stir up flares.

Smoke-free (Crohn’s)

Smoking makes Crohn’s worse. Get help to quit.

Skip NSAIDs

Ibuprofen and naproxen can trigger flares. Ask what to take instead.

Provider checkKeep taking your medicines.

  • Labs first. Your provider may check vitamin D, iron, B12 and folate, and your bones if you take steroids. With the shakes this plan gives 5,800 IU of vitamin D a day, over the 4,000 IU upper limit: your provider sets the dose and how long to take it.
  • Looser stools on days 1 to 10? Inflam-Eze gives 520 mg of magnesium a day (supplement limit 350 mg): tell your provider before you count it as a flare.
  • On warfarin or another blood thinner? Skip D3 5000 with K2, and ask your provider before the shakes: both have turmeric.
  • On ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin or doxycycline? Take it 2 hours before or 6 hours after a shake: the shake’s calcium, magnesium, zinc and iron block it.
  • Weak immunity or a central IV line? Many IBD medicines calm the immune system: ask your provider before UltraBiotic Defense, whose S. boulardii is a live yeast.
  • Pregnant or nursing? Ask your provider before you start any of these.
  • Beef or red-meat (alpha-gal) allergy? Skip GI Defend, and ask your provider before D3 5000 with K2: its softgel shell is gelatin.
  • Smoker or ex-smoker? Ask your provider before GI Restore: it is high in beta-carotene, which raised lung cancer risk in smokers.
  • Fever, heavy bleeding or a flare that won’t settle? Call your gastroenterologist right away, or go to the emergency room.
