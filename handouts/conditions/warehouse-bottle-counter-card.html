Ask your pharmacist

Before you grab the
warehouse bottle

Big-box and warehouse vitamins can be a fair deal. Four things on the label decide what you are really paying for. Check them, or let us check them with you.

1 · Count the pills

Dose per pill, not per bottle

A typical “1,000 mg fish oil” softgel holds only 250 to 300 mg of EPA and DHA, the omega-3s that do the work. Read the panel, not the front.

2 · Read the form

Not all magnesium is the same

Many low-cost bottles use magnesium oxide: cheap to make, poorly absorbed and the form most likely to upset your stomach. Chelated magnesium (glycinate, malate) is gentler and better used by the body.

3 · Look for active forms

Ready-to-use B vitamins

Most mass-market multis use folic acid and cyanocobalamin, which your body has to convert. Methylfolate (L-5-MTHF) and methylcobalamin arrive ready to use, which matters for the many people who convert poorly.

4 · Check it against your medicines

The one thing an aisle can't do

Some supplements and prescriptions need spacing or swapping. Doxycycline must be taken 2 to 3 hours apart from magnesium, calcium, iron and zinc. Acid blockers can drain magnesium and B12. Statins can lower CoQ10. We check your list for free.

“I can get it at Costco.”
The 60-second answer

Six moves, in order. Win on dose, form and the pharmacist, never on knocking the store.

1
Agree first

Costco has great prices, and a lot of their store brand is USP-verified. I'm not going to knock it. Respect earns the next 45 seconds.

2
Count the pills

How many of those do you take to get your dose? Turn the bottle around together. Fish oil is the easy one: four warehouse softgels to match one Omega Pure 1000.

3
Read the form

Magnesium oxide versus chelated glycinate. Folic acid and cyanocobalamin versus methylfolate and methylcobalamin. Point at the panel, not the price.

4
Check their medicines

Pull out the drug-induced nutrient depletion form. Which prescriptions are you on? This is the conversation the warehouse can never have.

5
Make it easy

In stock today, no shipping, stored right, vitamin club credit. If you ever run out, we have it this afternoon.

6
Leave the door open

If they still want the warehouse bottle: Bring it in next time and I'll check it against your meds, free. They come back, and so does the conversation.

Never say that warehouse vitamins are fake, junk or untested: many carry the USP Verified mark, and a customer who knows that stops trusting everything else you say. Never fight on price per bottle. Fight on price per effective dose.
